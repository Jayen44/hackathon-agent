# Legal basis for the careful-driver recovery engine (status at 15 Oct 2026)

Research note, not legal advice. Written by Claude from primary sources fetched on 15 Oct 2026. Needs review by a qualified UK lawyer before anything here goes into a customer contract or a report.

**Marks:** **V** = Verified: primary source fetched today and quoted. **S** = Secondary: law-firm, press or tracker source only, or a primary source that could not be fetched. **U** = Unverified: from memory or inference, so check it before use.

## 1. Executive summary (one screen, for the pitch)

**Safe to say on stage**
1. "UK law already sets the benchmark we test against. The Automated Vehicles Act 2024 says self-driving vehicles must reach a level of safety *equivalent to, or higher than, that of careful and competent human drivers*." (s.2(2)(a), **V**). The new UN ADS Regulation uses the same idea: "at least to the level of a competent and careful human driver" (draft para 5.1, **V**). UK careless and dangerous driving law has long measured drivers against "a competent and careful driver" (RTA 1988 ss.2A, 3ZA, **V**).
2. "Under the 2018 Act, when a self-driving vehicle causes an accident, the insurer pays the victim first (s.2). Once the victim's claim is settled, the insurer can recover from anyone else liable, e.g. the manufacturer (s.5). The insurer has **two years** from settlement to sue (Limitation Act 1980 s.10A)." (**V**)
3. "Nobody defines the careful driver as a model you can run. The UK's draft Statement of Safety Principles (consultation June to Sept 2026) and the UN ADS Regulation both leave the definition open. Only UN R157 Annex 3 publishes reference models, and we run both of them as a bracket." (**V**)
4. "We use only public data: NHTSA SGO crash reports, which NHTSA itself says may be incomplete or unverified." (**V**)

**Must NOT say**
1. ~~"Today's London robotaxis are covered by the 2018 Act."~~ The government's list of self-driving vehicles still says "there are no self-driving vehicles listed for use in Great Britain" (gov.uk, **V**), and s.2 applies only to listed vehicles. The Uber + Wayve London service (live since Sept 2026) runs under TfL private-hire licensing with a licensed driver on board (**S**). Under s.8(1)(a) a vehicle is "driving itself" only if it "does not need to be monitored", so that service is outside s.2. Today, recovery is the ordinary route: claims against the PHV driver or operator, then contribution or product-liability claims against Wayve. The s.2/s.5 route switches on when vehicles are listed or (after AVA 2024 commencement) authorised.
2. ~~"Our models are the legal standard."~~ R157 Annex 3 is headed "Guidance". It is scoped to ALKS on motorway-type roads up to 130 km/h, and its models describe "attentive human drivers' behaviour with ADAS". No UK court or regulator has adopted them as the civil-liability test. Say "a published regulatory reference model".
3. ~~"The AV Act 2024 is in force."~~ Only ss.55, 56, 78 (partly), 82-90 (APS permits, from 15 May 2026) and s.93 are in force, plus ss.94-100. Authorisation, the safety principles, user-in-charge immunity, operator licensing, the inspectorate and the change from "listed" to "authorised" are all still prospective (**V**). DfT reporting says full implementation is expected in the second half of 2027 (**S**).
4. ~~"The UN ADS regulation is in force."~~ It was notified on 30 July 2026. It is adopted unless more than one fifth of Contracting Parties object within six months, so it cannot enter into force before late January 2027 (**V**).
5. ~~"The AV caused / is liable / was defective."~~ Say "recovery case" and "triage evidence". Every pound figure is an expected value with a spread, not a liability finding.
6. ~~"Insurers have a legal right to the AV's data."~~ No such duty is in force. The Law Commission recommended one (Rec 74). AVA s.14 and s.88 only *allow* future authorisation or permit conditions to require sharing "with private businesses (such as vehicle manufacturers or insurers)", and s.14 is not in force (**V**). The route today is pre-action disclosure under CPR 31.16.

Product point: the PM1 parameters describe an **alert, trained driver who is expecting the event** (second-trial 0.4 s, trained-driver 0.774 G, ADAS assist). That makes "a careful human would have avoided it" easier to show. Defence counsel will attack exactly this, and it is why PM2 and a placeholder ordinary-driver sensitivity (e.g. 0.8 s judgement, 0.689 G) should be reported alongside. Olleja et al. (Chalmers, 2025) found these models are not validated against real safety-critical human behaviour (**S**).

## 3. Implications for the product (concrete changes)

1. **Benchmark wording.** Use "careful and competent human driver (AVA 2024 s.2(2)(a)); reference models: UN R157 01 series Annex 3 Performance models 1 and 2 (guidance)". Never call it "the legal standard" or "UK-approved".
2. **Regime field per claim.** Add `regime ∈ {AEVA_s2_listed, AEVA_s2_authorised (post-commencement), conventional_PHV_or_trial}` and `recovery_route ∈ {AEVA_s5, contribution_1978, CPA_direct, policy_s4}`. In the UK today every claim is `conventional` (no listed vehicles, and the London pilot has a safety driver). US SGO crashes are demo material: "proven on real US crashes, built for the UK regime" holds only if this is shown.
3. **The recovery case is derivative (AEVA s.5).** The adjuster's P(success) must be P(the manufacturer or other party is liable to the victim), with P(careful driver avoids) as one input, not the whole. Cap recovery at that party's liability to the victim (s.5(4)) and at the payout (s.5(3)).
4. **Claim-value scope.** Exclude the AV's own damage, goods carried for hire and the insured's own property (AEVA s.2(3)). Cap property at the RTA s.145(4)(b) figure. For the CPA route, drop non-private property and claims of £275 or less (CPA s.5). Apply contributory negligence (s.3(1)).
5. **R157 coverage vs urban robotaxi gaps.** R157 covers cut-in, cut-out and lead deceleration on divided, pedestrian-free roads up to 130 km/h, for M/N1 vehicles. Urban robotaxi crashes in the SGO (junctions, turning, pedestrians, cyclists, oncoming traffic, parking manoeuvres, being rear-ended while stopped) are outside it. Tag every result with `r157_scenario_in_scope` and `r157_road_context_in_scope`, and drop confidence when either is false. Use the rule-based Highway-Code careful driver (labelled) for the rest.
6. **Model bias disclosure.** PM1 (JAMA CCDM) describes an alert, trained, ADAS-assisted driver. Add a "typical driver" sensitivity row (placeholder values with TODO and citation needed) and let defence counsel argue it.
7. **s.4 exclusions checklist in the claim bundle:** software version at crash, outstanding safety-critical OTA updates and whether the insured knew of them, unauthorised software changes. If any is present, the recovery target becomes the insured, not the manufacturer.
8. **s.3(2) check:** was the ADS engaged outside its ODD by the person in charge? Use SGO "Within ODD?" and note when it is redacted as CBI.
9. **Limitation clocks (put them in the UI):** AEVA s.5 action within 2 years of settlement (LA s.10A). Contribution within 2 years of judgment or agreed payment (LA s.10). CPA within 3 years of knowledge, with a 10-year longstop from supply (LA s.11A). Show "days to deadline" on each claim.
10. **Data access route.** "Pull full logs first" should produce (a) a preservation letter (cite LC Rec 73: 39 months), (b) a CPR 31.16 application summary listing data classes (R157 DSSAD occurrence flags paras 8.2.x, ADS engagement and transition demand timeline, OTA history), (c) for APS-permitted services, a request for any s.88 permit data-sharing condition. Never assert a statutory right to the data.
11. **Disclaimers on every report:** "Triage evidence for a claims handler; not legal advice or a liability finding. Counterfactual uses published UN R157 Annex 3 guidance models outside their motorway scope where flagged. Source data are unverified NHTSA SGO reports (US), which NHTSA says may be incomplete, duplicated and not normalised. UK legal regime per AEVA 2018 / AVA 2024 as at <date>; most of AVA 2024 is not yet in force."
12. **Language.** Do not use "self-driving" for unauthorised vehicles (AVA s.78, in force for specified purposes). Use "ADS-engaged". No accusatory wording about named companies.
13. **EU expansion slide.** PLD 2024/2853 (software as a product, disclosure, presumptions) from 9 Dec 2026. Mark it "S: verify on EUR-Lex".
14. **UN ADS Regulation positioning.** From ~2027 manufacturers must document how they define the careful driver per territory, so the insurer needs an independent check of that definition. That is our pitch line, and the regulation is not yet in force.

## 4. Open questions for a lawyer
