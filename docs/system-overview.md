# System overview: how a crash becomes a decision

One crash in, four answers out: who pays (human driver or tech company), what the insurer can recover, whether to pursue, and what to do next. UK law, synthetic data, decision support only (not a legal finding). Read `coord/HANDOFF.md` first for current state; this file explains the machinery.

**Owners:** `insurance/` Jayen (liability, counterfactual, bridge, resolution) · `finance/` Antonio (pricing, pursue rule) · `sim/` Vakhtang (physics and R157 driver models, on branch `sim`) · `agents/` and the dashboard page: Antonio.

```
crash bundle (JSON) -> integrity -> liability engine -> bridge -> finance.assess -> resolution (outcome + steps)
                                         ^ counterfactual estimator(s)                 ^ defence-counsel debate (agents/)
```

## 1. Input: the crash bundle (`insurance/bundle.py`)
Validated JSON. Sections:
- **Vehicle:** mode timeline (manual / assisted / automated), occupant, handover demand and response times, driver-monitoring samples, control inputs (source, brake), faults, software version and approved list, ODD flag, AEVA s.3(2) and s.4 flags, `software_verified`.
- **Perception:** sensors, detected objects with first-detection times, decision-log present.
- **External:** CCTV, witnesses, police report, weather, lighting.
- **Integrity:** hashes, chain of custody, clock offsets, log gaps, tamper flag.
- **Conflict (the reconstruction):** hazard, `t_start` (when a human could first see it), ego speed, gap, hazard speed, friction, kind (stopped hazard, lead braking, cut-in).
- **Outcome:** collided, impact speed, injury severity, optional paid amount, optional injured-party contributory-negligence share.
- **Legal (optional):** `regime`, `recovery_route`, settlement / knowledge / supply dates, `p_liable_to_victim` [low, mid, high].

The 22 examples in `insurance/examples` are generated by `scripts/make_examples.py`; `settle()` computes each timeline (impact time and speed) from the logged braking so the data is self-consistent. Edit the script, never the JSON.

## 2. Step one: who is at fault (`insurance/engine.py`, `decide`)
A fixed sequence. The first check that applies decides the case; each decision records a coded step so the path is visible.
1. **No collision:** `NO_FAULT_DRIVING`.
2. **Integrity:** tamper flag, no hashes, clock offset over 0.25 s, or a log gap at the crash blocks the case (`NEEDS_REVIEW`, no liability decision).
3. **Control state:** manual or assisted is `HUMAN_DRIVER` (ordinary motor claim). A human input within 2 s of the crash while automated is `HUMAN_DRIVER`, flagged for review.
4. **Statutory flags:** AEVA s.3(2) (engaged when inappropriate) is recorded as an exclusion for the person in charge only. AEVA s.4 (unauthorised software change or missed safety-critical update) ends as `HUMAN_DRIVER`: recover from the insured, not the manufacturer.
5. **Software** not on the approved list: `TECH_COMPANY`.
6. **Outside the ODD:** `TECH_COMPANY`.
7. **Critical fault** within 60 s of the crash: `TECH_COMPANY`.
8. **Handover:** human took control 2 s or more before the crash: `HUMAN_DRIVER`; warning of 10 s or more that the human ignored: `HUMAN_DRIVER`; warning shorter than 10 s: `TECH_COMPANY`.
9. **Careful-driver test:** would a careful, competent human have avoided it? P(avoid) of 80% or more is `TECH_COMPANY`; 20% or less is `NO_FAULT_DRIVING`; in between is borderline and goes to human review.

Thresholds (80% / 20%, 10 s, 2 s) are illustrative policy values, not law.

**The estimator** (`insurance/counterfactual.py`) is a plug point. Today it is a placeholder: straight-line braking, reaction time sampled from 0.7 / 1.0 / 1.5 s, numeric integration for lead-braking and cut-in. It reports P(avoid) and whether a fast, median or slow human would avoid. The real estimator is Vakhtang's UN R157 Annex 3 models (CCDM and FSM) in `sim/`; `sim_adapter.py` is the seam, not wired until `sim/` merges.

## 3. Step two: the bridge (`insurance/bridge.py`)
The only place that touches both `insurance/` and `finance/`. It stops early, with a reason, on: no collision, failed integrity, human liable, no reconstructed conflict, or invalid claim inputs. Otherwise it:
1. Runs each estimator; each gives P(avoid) with a 90% interval (sampling error only). Two estimators bracket the result.
2. Scales by `p_liable_to_victim` when set (AEVA s.5 recovery is derivative of the victim's claim). Unset is flagged in the notes.
3. Translates facts into finance's UK codes (engagement, ODD, software; "compliant" needs positive confirmation).
4. Adds regime notes (a "conventional" regime has no listed vehicle, so s.5 pricing is a proxy) and the limitation deadline from `insurance/legal.py` (s.5: 2 years from settlement; contribution: 2 years; CPA 1987: 3 years from knowledge with a 10-year longstop).

## 4. Step three: pricing and the pursue decision (`finance/recovery.py`)
- **AV share** = P(avoid) x (1 - injured party's contributory negligence). A paid amount is already net of it, so it is not applied twice.
- **Claim value:** the paid amount if known, else a table by severity (Slight / Serious / Fatal / Damage only; Serious and Fatal are general damages only).
- **Legal cost:** CPR PD 45 fixed recoverable costs (low, central, high). Above GBP 100k there is no sourced figure, so `cannot_price` unless a paid amount or counsel budget is supplied.
- **Expected recovery** = AV share x value - legal cost, as a low / mid / high range. Opt-in `cost_shift_risk` (CPR 44.2(2)) subtracts the other side's costs, assumed equal to ours, from the worst case.
- **Decision:** `not_av_recovery` (not engaged, or s.4 breach); `cannot_price`; `dont_pursue` (best case does not clear the threshold); `pursue` (central case clears it and no gate is open; a negative worst case is stated as `LITIGATION_DOWNSIDE`: open with a pre-action letter only); `pull_logs_first` (a gate is open: engagement unverified, ODD outside or unknown, simulation not reproduced, one model only, models either side of 50% `MODELS_DISAGREE`, or the range straddles the threshold). Contributory negligence not assessed is a caveat, not a gate.
- Inputs are validated (paid amount > 0, legal cost 0 < low <= high, share in 0..1). Output carries machine-readable `codes`; callers never parse prose.

## 5. Step four: resolution (`insurance/resolution.py`)
Every crash lands in exactly one outcome, with ordered next steps naming who acts and what each step unblocks.

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CLOSE_NO_CLAIM` | No collision |
| `HUMAN_REVIEW` | Evidence cannot be trusted; fix and re-run |
| `MOTOR_CLAIM` | Human at fault; ordinary claim |
| `SETTLE_NO_RECOVERY` | Not worth pursuing |
| `RECOVER_FROM_INSURED` | Not engaged, or s.4 breach |
| `PULL_LOGS` | Gated; steps name the gaps |
| `NEEDS_INPUTS` | Supply a paid amount or counsel budget |
| `PURSUE` | Open recovery (headline says "pre-action only" when the worst case is a lost trial) |

Steps are chosen from finance's codes: preservation letter for the vehicle logs then pre-action disclosure (CPR 31.16; no statutory right to the data), re-run the reconstruction, assess contributory negligence, run a second model, resolve model disagreement, confirm the recovery route. For open claims the limitation deadline is the first step (URGENT under 90 days), and a step asks counsel to assess `p_liable_to_victim` when unset. A test fails if finance adds a code with no step.

## 6. Defence-counsel debate (`agents/debate.py`)
An adjuster prices the claim, defence counsel finds the weakest assumption and files the strongest challenge, and the adjuster rebuts. Every number comes from `finance/`; agents only pick inputs to test, and `apply_change` keeps challenges within what the evidence allows. The final decision is recomputed from the accepted inputs; a rejected challenge that would flip the decision marks the claim `CONTESTED` for a human. Runs on Claude (API key) or offline with scripted stand-ins; results show in `dashboard/pages/1_Agent_debates.py`.

## 7. Running it
```
python -m insurance <bundle.json> [--price]       one crash; --price adds recovery and the outcome
python -m insurance.portfolio --n 20 --seed 7     synthetic crashes, ranked recoverability map in GBP
python -m insurance.portfolio --dir insurance/examples
python -m insurance.gaps                          decision paths with no example
python scripts/sensitivity.py                     how reaction-time assumptions move the verdict
python -m pytest -q
```
`--price` on one bundle uses one estimator and `reproduced=False`, so it stops at `pull_logs_first`; the portfolio run uses two estimators and reaches `pursue`.
