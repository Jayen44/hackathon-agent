Why the United States first. Waymo runs about 500,000 paid rides a week in 10 cities, roughly 200 million driverless miles a year: that is where real driverless claims are. Tesla's supervised driving system has 8 billion miles on Tesla's own figures: volume for screening, though liability sits mostly with the driver, so we sell triage there, not guaranteed recovery. The United States also publishes crash reports under a federal reporting order, which the regulator says may be incomplete, and public real-crash studies to validate against: our simulator reproduces 88 percent of crashes and 94 percent of near-crashes in the 100-Car study. The United Kingdom has 15 supervised vehicles and none on the government list, so little volume until its regime is live, expected late 2027 from secondary reporting. Scoring cues: business viability, pitch.
