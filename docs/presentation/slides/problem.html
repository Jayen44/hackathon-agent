Few claims, each contested, and the proof sits with the other side

Few, but high-stakes

Over 25 million driverless miles, one insurer study found 88% fewer property-damage and 92% fewer injury claims than human drivers. The ones that remain are contested.

The proof is theirs

The driving logs sit with the operator or manufacturer. The insurer must request or compel them before it knows whether a claim is worth pursuing.

Recovery is a real market

US insurers filed about $27bn of subrogation demands in 2025. Recovery firms keep 15% to 33%.

Two expensive mistakes: chasing a claim that fails, and missing one that would succeed. A fast, evidenced first read prevents both.

Sources: Swiss Re and Waymo study (25.3 million miles); Arbitration Forums 2025 (all auto, not driverless only). market/MARKET.md. Automated Vehicle Recovery Desk

Why it is needed. Driverless vehicles crash far less often, so there are few claims, and each one is contested and high-stakes. The evidence is held by the party you would be chasing. Recovery is a large market: about 27 billion dollars of subrogation demands were filed in the US in 2025 across all auto claims, with recovery firms keeping 15 to 33 percent. Two mistakes cost money: chasing a claim that fails, and missing one that would succeed. A fast, evidenced first read prevents both. Scoring cue: business viability, a real problem (20%).
