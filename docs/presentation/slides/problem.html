The problem

The insurer pays first. Getting the money back comes later, and it is hard.

1. Pay first

When a self-driving vehicle causes an accident, its insurer pays the victim, with no wait to settle fault (section 2).

2. Recover later

The insurer may then claim from the manufacturer or another liable party, once the victim’s claim is settled (section 5).

3. The proof is theirs

The driving logs sit with the automated vehicle company. No law gives the insurer a right to them, and the clock runs two years from settlement.

Sources: Automated and Electric Vehicles Act 2018, sections 2 and 5; Limitation Act 1980, section 10A. Automated Vehicle Recovery Desk

Three beats. The insurer pays the victim first, so the victim is protected whatever we do. Recovery is a later, separate step against the company behind the automated vehicle. And the evidence to prove it is held by the party you are chasing, with no legal right to it, and a two-year limit that loses the claim if missed. Scoring cue: business viability, a real problem (20%).
