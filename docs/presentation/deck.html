Tesla's supervised driving system has 8 billion miles on Tesla's own figures: volume for screening, though liability sits mostly with the driver, so we sell triage there, not guaranteed recovery. Walk the six steps. Intake: the crash bundle arrives and passes an integrity gate; tampering, clock drift or log gaps stop the case. Liability: who was in control, the software version, the handover, any faults. Careful driver: the simulator runs two published reference drivers, A and B, and checks it can reproduce the reported crash. Pricing: recoverable money as a range after legal cost. Outcome: one of eight, with named next steps, an owner and the limitation deadline first. Sign-off: a named human approves the spend. Then the loop: new evidence re-runs the claim and the ledger shows what changed. Code produces every number; the agents only challenge it. Scoring cues: demo clarity, architecture.

How the agents interact for long-term running. The portfolio lead takes the next claim from the queue and hands it to the investigator and the reconstructor in parallel, then to the counterfactual analyst, then calls the insurance engine and records a finding; it reuses the same three worker threads for every claim. Long runs are made safe by: a queue with a budget cap; reused worker threads; a limit of five reconstruction attempts after which the claim is flagged low confidence rather than forced; a debate in which an adjuster prices the claim, defence counsel argues for the other side and the adjuster rebuts, with a claim marked CONTESTED and sent to a human if a rejected challenge would flip the decision; and a trace and ledger of every run so a restart or a re-run shows what changed. Agents never produce numbers; the finance engine recomputes every figure. Scoring cue: agent design and architecture (15%).
